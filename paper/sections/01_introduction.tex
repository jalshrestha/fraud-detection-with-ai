\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In July 2023, the Curve Finance exploit drained \$61M in seconds by
exploiting a reentrancy flaw buried in three lines of Vyper bytecode---a
vulnerability invisible to any transaction-graph detector yet unambiguous
in a static analysis of the contract source~\cite{defihacklabs2024}.
Two years earlier, the Poly Network attacker moved nearly \$600M across
chains using an address that received transfers from hundreds of protocol
wallets within a single hour---a structural anomaly invisible to any
code-only scanner, yet unmistakable in the transaction graph.
These two incidents encode the central thesis of this paper:
\emph{on-chain fraud detection requires both modalities simultaneously.}
Neither the transaction graph nor the contract source code, examined alone,
can detect the full spectrum of Ethereum fraud.

Financial fraud on Ethereum---phishing, Ponzi schemes, rug-pulls, and
smart-contract exploits---inflicts more than \$1 billion in annual
losses~\cite{chainalysis2023}.
Supervised classifiers trained on hand-crafted
features~\cite{ostapowicz2019,blanco2022,ashfaq2022,elmougy2023} were
the first generation of automated defences; they improved on manual
heuristics but failed to generalise to novel attack patterns.
The second generation turned to graph neural networks (GNNs), modelling
blockchains as transaction networks~\cite{kim2023,zhou2021,ctgcn2024}
and achieving strong results on known fraud typologies.
Parallel work in smart-contract vulnerability analysis used symbolic
execution and deep learning on bytecode to detect reentrancy, unchecked
calls, and honeypots~\cite{luu2016oyente,tsankov2018securify,
sun2024gptscan}.
Yet both generations remain single-modal: graph methods are blind to the
semantic intent hidden in contract bytecode, and code methods ignore the
network context that reveals whether a contract is acting in isolation or
coordinating with other fraud infrastructure.

The third generation---hybrid fusion models---has begun to close this
gap.  BERT4ETH~\cite{hu2023bert4eth} treats Ethereum transaction
histories as natural-language sentences and pre-trains a Transformer
encoder over them, capturing sequential behavioural patterns but lacking
any direct access to contract source semantics.
TLMG4Eth~\cite{sun2025tlmg4eth} jointly learns from transaction language
and graph structure, reporting strong gains on its own corpus.
PonziGuard~\cite{liang2025ponziguard} couples contract logic with fund-flow
dynamics in a runtime behaviour graph.
Each of these advances the frontier, but none combines
\emph{verified victim-contract source code} with a \emph{reproducible,
openly seeded transaction graph}, and none reports a comprehensive
module-level ablation that isolates the contribution of each architectural
choice.  The success of transformer-based classifiers across
text-classification tasks~\cite{poudel2026fakenews} and AI-driven
intrusion detection systems~\cite{miya2024indekx} further motivates
extending deep neural fusion to the on-chain domain.

\textbf{This paper.}
We present a \emph{hybrid GNN-LLM fusion architecture} that processes
the Ethereum transaction graph through a two-layer GraphSAGE
network~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} and verified Solidity source code
through a frozen CodeBERT encoder~\cite{feng2020codebert} in parallel,
fusing the two 64- and 768-dimensional representations through a learned
128-dimensional bottleneck.  The architecture directly addresses the
limitations above:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Structural blindness of code-only methods.}
  GraphSAGE aggregates eight per-node features encoding degree,
  transaction-value flow, and unique-counterparty counts, capturing the
  network signatures of phishing campaigns and coordinated fund extraction
  that are absent from source code.

  \item \textbf{Semantic blindness of graph-only methods.}
  CodeBERT encodes the full Solidity source, surfacing reentrancy traps,
  unbounded-mint backdoors, and logic vulnerabilities that leave no
  distinctive transactional footprint.

  \item \textbf{Label quality through victim-first seeding.}
  All 156 primary fraud seeds are \emph{victim contracts} from
  DeFiHackLabs proof-of-concept exploit files~\cite{defihacklabs2024},
  each verified to carry Solidity source on Etherscan and confirmed
  on-chain transaction history.  This strategy, described in
  Section~\ref{subsec:collection}, produces fraud labels of high
  confidence and ensures full multi-modal coverage for every labelled
  address.

  \item \textbf{Reproducibility through explicit seed arrays.}
  The two seed arrays (\texttt{FRAUD\_ADDRESSES} and
  \texttt{BENIGN\_ADDRESSES}) are first-class artefacts of the codebase.
  Every downstream step---BigQuery sampling, DuckDB integration, graph
  construction, model training---is deterministic given the same seeds.
\end{itemize}

On a held-out test split of 47 contracts drawn from a graph of
\num{85680} nodes and \num{239742} transactions, the fusion model achieves
\textbf{accuracy 93.6\%}, \textbf{fraud F1-score 0.909},
\textbf{AUC-ROC 0.961}, and \textbf{AUC-PR 0.959}---the highest
F1-Fraud and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC\,=\,0.861) across all
seven evaluated models.

\textbf{Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A reproducible multi-modal data pipeline seeding from
  156 DeFiHackLabs victim contracts and 504 blue-chip benign contracts,
  expanded via Google BigQuery and augmented with Forta Network fraud
  labels and Etherscan Solidity source, yielding a labelled graph with
  eight engineered node features per address.

  \item A hybrid GNN-LLM fusion classifier that couples GraphSAGE
  structural embeddings with frozen CodeBERT semantic embeddings through
  a concatenation bottleneck, achieving the best balanced fraud-detection
  performance on our benchmark.

  \item A comprehensive seven-model ablation study---GraphSAGE, GCN,
  GAT, CodeBERT-only, attention-based fusion, Random Forest, and the
  proposed Concat-Fusion---that empirically validates each architectural
  and feature-engineering choice.

  \item An honest evaluation including explicit discussion of random-split
  optimism, label-noise sensitivity, and code-coverage constraints, with
  a concrete roadmap for deployment-grade validation.
\end{enumerate}
