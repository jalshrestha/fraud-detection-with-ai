\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The central claim of this paper is that Ethereum smart-contract fraud
detection requires both modalities simultaneously: the transaction graph
to capture \emph{who interacts with whom}, and the contract source code
to understand \emph{what those interactions do}.  Our experiments confirm
this claim.  No single-modality baseline---graph encoder, code encoder,
or Random Forest---achieves the F1-Fraud and MCC of the fusion model.

We presented a hybrid GNN-LLM fusion architecture that couples a
two-layer GraphSAGE network, operating on eight engineered node features
encoding degree, value flow, and counterparty statistics, with a frozen
CodeBERT encoder over verified Solidity source code.  A reproducible
four-phase pipeline---BigQuery transaction sampling, DuckDB integration,
PyTorch Geometric graph construction, and CodeBERT tokenisation---produces
a labelled graph of \num{85680} nodes and \num{239742} transactions,
seeded from 156 DeFiHackLabs victim contracts (94.5\% of fraud labels)
and 504 blue-chip benign contracts.  Of the 85,680 addresses in the
graph, 304 carry both verified source code and an explicit label; these
are the only samples used for supervised training and evaluation.

On the held-out test split, the concatenation-based fusion model
achieves \textbf{F1-Fraud\,=\,0.909}, \textbf{AUC-ROC\,=\,0.961}, and
\textbf{MCC\,=\,0.861}---the highest values across all seven evaluated
models.  The comprehensive seven-model ablation study yields three
empirical findings beyond the headline number.
First, the eight engineered node features are highly discriminative on
their own: a Random Forest over raw features achieves
F1\,=\,0.889 and AUC-ROC\,=\,0.988, validating the feature-engineering
effort.  Second, GraphSAGE's neighbour-sampling aggregation is more
stable than GCN or GAT on our small, directed transaction graph: GAT
degenerates entirely at this training scale.  Third, concatenation-based
fusion outperforms attention-based fusion (F1\,=\,0.625) because learnable
cross-modal attention weights are under-specified at 212 training samples.

The modular codebase, explicit seed arrays, and deterministic pipeline
released with this paper provide a verifiable baseline for future work.

\textbf{Future work} should address the following priorities:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evaluation hardening.}  The current random split is
    optimistic: sibling contracts from a single campaign can straddle the
    split boundary.  Temporal and deployer-disjoint splits are required
    for deployment-grade evaluation.  Results should also be averaged
    over at least five random seeds and reported with bootstrap confidence
    intervals to assess statistical significance of the fusion-vs-ablation
    gaps.  Evaluation on a shared public benchmark---such as the Ethereum
    phishing dataset used by TTAGN~\cite{li2022ttagn} and
    BERT4ETH~\cite{hu2023bert4eth}---would enable direct comparison with
    published systems.

    \item \textbf{Dataset expansion.}  Fraud recall is bounded by the
    small count of labelled fraud contracts with verified source (95 in
    training).  Incorporating post-2026 DeFiHackLabs exploits, decompiled
    bytecode for unverified contracts, and additional fraud categories from
    Forta would likely raise the fraud-recall ceiling more than further
    architectural changes.

    \item \textbf{Parameter-efficient LLM fine-tuning.}
    LoRA adapters~\cite{hu2022lora} would allow CodeBERT to specialise
    on Solidity vulnerability patterns---reentrancy signatures, access
    control patterns, unbounded mint logic---within the memory budget of
    a consumer GPU.  The frozen-CodeBERT result provides a strong
    baseline for measuring the gain from fine-tuning.

    \item \textbf{Temporal graph modelling.}
    The static GraphSAGE encoder cannot distinguish the bootstrapping
    phase of a fraud contract (when funds are accumulating) from its
    drain phase (when funds are extracted).  Replacing GraphSAGE with a
    temporal graph network~\cite{wu2024tokenscout} that encodes edge
    timestamps would let the model capture this lifecycle.

    \item \textbf{Cross-chain generalisation.}  Testing the pipeline on
    BNB Chain, Polygon, and Arbitrum would reveal whether the fusion
    architecture---and the victim-first seeding strategy in
    particular---generalises across EVM-compatible networks with
    different gas economics, liquidity patterns, and DeFi ecosystems.
\end{enumerate}

On-chain fraud is not a static target.  Adversaries adapt, new attack
vectors emerge, and the contracts that encode them evolve.  A detector
that fuses structural and semantic analysis, grounded in reproducible,
openly seeded data, provides the most defensible foundation we can build
with today's evidence.
